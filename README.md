# best flight deals: how to find cheap fares, when to book, and where cashback stacks on top

Airfare is the one purchase where the price you see is a snapshot, not a fact. Katy Nastro of Going puts it plainly: airfare is volatile, "it's not ever a singular price." Expedia's Melanie Fish points at demand, competition, fuel costs and seasonality as the levers, and adds that when airlines notice empty seats — or a low-cost carrier undercutting them — they move.

That volatility has been worse than usual lately. CPI data showed airfares up roughly 20% year over year as of April. Which means the useful question isn't "which site has the best flight deals?" It's "what sequence of decisions gets me the lowest price I can actually book today?"

Here's what the current data says about that sequence, and the one layer most flight searches skip entirely.

## Why no single site wins at this

Flight comparison sites mostly crawl the same inventory. Google Flights shows you the lowest fare it found for your date; if another booking site has the same seat cheaper, Google Flights often won't tell you. That's price dispersion, and it's normal — the same flight gets priced differently across booking sites on the same day.

The size of the spread varies by route and timing. In one ShopBack test comparing Google Flights against its own comparison tool across six bookings, comparing across booking sites surfaced differences of $5 to $40 per booking on flights, and hotel spreads were wider. A smaller win than the marketing implies, but a real one.

Deal trackers show the same picture at scale. Kayak's Monthly Flight Deal Tracker, which rounds up lowest average round-trip fares from more than 30 US airports, recently showed Boston–Riga at $558 round trip — down 35% from July's average — while the cheapest domestic route, Atlanta–Jacksonville, averaged $55.

## When to book, according to the data that exists

Four credible studies give four different answers, which is your first clue that these are tendencies, not rules.

| Source | Domestic flights | International flights |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Expedia 2026 Air Hacks | 15–30 days out, about $130 cheaper than booking 180+ days ahead | 31–45 days out saves $190 on average; 8–14 days out saves $225 |
| Google (2025 research) | Lowest average prices 39 days before departure | 49 days or more; 48 for Europe, 50 for Mexico and the Caribbean |
| Going's "Goldilocks Window" | 1–3 months out off-season, 3–7 months for peak summer and holidays | 2–8 months off-season, 3.5–9 months for peak periods |
| Hotels.com (via NYT, Aug 2026) | Last-minute bookers saved an average 23% versus booking 4+ months ahead; sweet spot 8–14 days | Same pattern |

The overlap is narrow but real: booking six or more months out is rarely the cheapest move, and the last ten days before departure usually get expensive. Holiday travel adds its own shape — Kayak's 2026 holiday analysis found Thanksgiving fares historically bottom out about five weeks before the holiday, while Christmas fares dip from mid-November through early December. Waiting until the week of Thanksgiving can cost you 16% more than the prior week.

One rule that survives: two one-way tickets sometimes beat a round trip. Kayak's holiday data puts the saving at up to 30% on international routes and 29% on domestic ones.

> These windows shift with demand and fuel. If you're at the edge of one, book — the fare is more likely to rise than fall at that point.

## Days, months and airports that actually move the price

Expedia's 2026 Air Hacks report, its tenth, flagged a genuine change: Friday is now the cheapest day to book *and* to fly internationally, about 8% below Sunday, because business travel now drains out earlier in the week. Domestically, Tuesday is still cheapest, up to 14% less than a Sunday departure.

The cheapest month to fly internationally is August — fares average 29% lower than December, roughly $120 per ticket. Domestically it's January, when post-holiday demand collapses. If crowds matter more to you than price, Tuesday is the least busy day, February the least busy month, and February 25, March 4 and November 18 came out as the calmest individual dates. May 22, July 3 and August 29 were the worst.

Your departure airport matters more than most people assume. Fort Lauderdale, Las Vegas and Orlando price about 25% below the national average; Washington Dulles, San Francisco and JFK are the most expensive major airports to leave from.

## The tools, and what each one is genuinely good at

- **Google Flights** — calendar heat maps for flexible dates, the Explore map for "anywhere" searches, free price alerts. Still the best starting point for complex multi-leg routing.
- **Skyscanner** — set your destination to "everywhere" to see where your airport flies cheaply; price alerts with the Drops feature flagging 20%-plus drops.
- **Kayak** — monthly and holiday deal trackers that show you where fares are falling, which is useful when you don't care where you go.
- **Going, Dollar Flight Club and similar** — email alerts for mistake fares and drops. Going has a free tier; the best deals sit behind the paid one, which the company says saves members an average of $550 on economy tickets.
- **OTAs and bundles** — Expedia packages (flight plus hotel) discount roughly 8% to 15% versus booking separately, which often beats winning the fare war by a few dollars.
- **Direct with the airline** — the right call for Southwest, which most aggregators don't carry, and for anything involving elite status nights or award space.

## What flights actually earn

Flight cashback is lower than hotel cashback, because OTA margins on airfare are thinner. ShopBack's own US travel writing puts domestic US flights at roughly 1.5% to 3% and international flights at 3% to 6% depending on the booking site, with Trip.com flights typically earning 1.5% to 5% for US members. Across one six-booking test it documented, cashback ranged from $3 to $20 per flight booking.

Run the arithmetic on a $900 international fare: 4% back is $36, and 1.5% is $13.50. Not life-changing on one ticket. Across a family of four or a couple of trips a year, the difference between picking the 4% option and the 1.5% option becomes the price of a checked bag you didn't want to pay for in the first place.

Some mechanics to know before you count on it:

- Cashback is calculated on the booking value **excluding taxes and fees**.
- It shows as Pending within about 48 hours, and confirms 60 to 90 days after your travel date for flights. Hotels confirm 30 to 60 days after checkout; activities confirm faster, 15 to 30 days after the experience.
- Cancel or no-show and the cashback reverses, because the OTA's commission reverses too.
- Award tickets earn nothing. ShopBack is a cash-price layer.

**Eligibility differs by merchant**, and this trips people up:

- Booking.com: stays and rentals earn; flights and Booking.basic reservations do not.
- Priceline: percentage back on most bookings, but a flat dollar amount on flights.
- Trip.com: check which booking types earn before assuming a flight qualifies.
- Direct airline bookings: usually no cashback at all.

One timing quirk worth knowing: travel cashback doesn't follow the retail calendar. Booking sites tend to upsize rates in **January**, when trip planning peaks, rather than on Black Friday. If you're flexible about when you book rather than when you fly, that's the window to watch.

## Keeping the cashback attached

Most failed cashback claims trace back to the same two habits. Get these right and you'll rarely have to chase anything.

1. **Start from the cashback platform, not the booking site.** Sign in, search the merchant or open the Planner, then click through. That click is what creates the referral your cashback attaches to.
2. **Use only coupon codes featured on the merchant's own cashback page.** A code scraped from a coupon site, an email, or a browser extension can be treated as an outside referral and void the whole payout — a $20 discount that quietly costs you $60 in cashback.
3. **Finish in one continuous session.** Don't bounce to another tab for reviews, don't open a retargeting ad, don't let the session expire, and turn off coupon extensions before checkout.
4. **Sign in to the OTA first** if a member rate or loyalty rate applies, then run the same sequence.
5. **If nothing shows as Pending within 48 hours**, file a missing cashback claim with your booking confirmation. This is routine, not a red flag.

If you have a miles-earning card, use it for the payment. Cashback and card rewards are funded by different parties — the OTA's affiliate budget and the card network's interchange — so one doesn't cancel the other. Cards that earn bonus rewards on travel are the obvious pick when the booking codes as travel.

## Where this approach stops being the right tool

Cashback is a layer, not a strategy. Four cases where you should skip it:

- **Complex multi-city itineraries.** Comparison tools built on OTA inventory are weaker than Google Flights or ITA Matrix at routing a three-stop trip. Plan the routing first, then price the segments.
- **Award redemptions.** If you're spending Ultimate Rewards or Membership Rewards points, go to the airline or transfer partner. No affiliate commission means no cashback.
- **Specific hotels for elite nights.** Some loyalty programs only credit elite-qualifying nights on direct bookings. If the night needs to count for status, book direct and do the maths yourself.
- **Niche inventory.** Comparison tools cover most inventory, not all of it. A ryokan, a family-run guesthouse or a villa listed on one small platform may never appear — book it where it lives.

## A booking sequence that holds up

1. **Lock your dates before you open anything.** Flexibility is the biggest single lever, and you can't use it if you haven't decided what's actually movable.
2. **Check the calendar.** Skim a month view for the cheapest three-day window around your dates. Pick two candidates.
3. **Set a price alert** on your route so you're not refreshing manually for six weeks.
4. **Compare with cashback attached.** Run the route through the Travel Planner and look at the effective price per option, not the list price. A slightly pricier itinerary with a higher rate can beat the cheapest one with none.
5. **Check the airline's own site** for the winner, especially Southwest, and for anything involving status or award space.
6. **Check bundle maths** if you also need a hotel. An Expedia package at 8% to 15% off the components often beats the fare difference you were chasing.
7. **Book through the tracked link, apply only featured codes, complete in one session.** Then confirm the pending entry exists before you forget about it.
